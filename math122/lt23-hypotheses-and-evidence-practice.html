Learning Target 23 · LT23

Hypotheses and Evidence

State null and alternative hypotheses using population parameters, select the test direction, and interpret supplied p-values and statistical decisions.

Quick Review

Population parametersUse \(\mu\) for a population mean and \(p\) for a population proportion. Do not use sample statistics in hypotheses.
Null and alternativeWrite \(H_0:\theta=\theta_0\). Use \(H_a:\theta<\theta_0\) (left), \(H_a:\theta>\theta_0\) (right), or \(H_a:\theta\ne\theta_0\) (two-tailed).
p-valueAssuming \(H_0\) and test assumptions are correct, the probability of a result at least as extreme as observed in the direction(s) of \(H_a\).
Decision at significance level \(\alpha\)If \(p\text{-value}\leq\alpha\), reject \(H_0\). Otherwise fail to reject \(H_0\).

Choose \(\alpha\) before seeing results. Rejecting gives evidence, not proof; failing to reject does not prove the null. Assume all tests meet their conditions. No test-statistic or p-value calculations are required.
