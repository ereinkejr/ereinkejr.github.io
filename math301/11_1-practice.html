Quick Reference

Measurement describes the size of an object by comparing a quantity with a unit, which is a reference amount.

Measurement
Comparing a quantity with a chosen unit to determine its numerical value.
Additivity
When parts are combined, the measure of the whole is the sum of the measures of its non-overlapping parts.
U.S. Customary System
A system of units familiar in the United States.
Metric System
The system of measurement used by most of the world.

Examples of units: length may be described in inches, feet, centimeters, or meters; area in square units; volume in cubic units. The appropriate unit depends on the object and quantity being described.

Check Your Understanding

When selecting a unit, first identify what is being measured: length, area, volume, or mass. Then choose a unit that is appropriate for the scale of the object. Remember that a measurement is a numerical comparison with a unit, not just a number by itself.

For example, saying a desk is “120” does not fully describe its length. Saying it is “120 centimeters” identifies both the numerical value and the unit.
