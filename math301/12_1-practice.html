Quick Reference

**Area of a rectangle** \[A=L\cdot W\] The area is the number of square units that cover the inside of the rectangle without gaps or overlaps.
**Square units and conversions** If a length conversion is multiplied by a factor, the corresponding area conversion uses the square of that factor. For example, \[1\text{ yd}=3\text{ ft}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1\text{ yd}^2=9\text{ ft}^2.\]

A 6-by-3 rectangle contains 18 unit squares, so its area is \(6\cdot3=18\) square units.

**Area of a rectangle with algebraic side lengths** \[(x+a)(x+b)=x^2+(a+b)x+ab.\] A rectangle model can show the four partial areas that make up this product (often called FOIL).

Area with a Border or Sidewalk

A common strategy for a rectangular border is to find the area of the larger rectangle and subtract the area of the inner rectangle:

\[A_{\text{border}}=A_{\text{outer}}-A_{\text{inner}}.\]

For a 8 ft by 12 ft garden surrounded by a 1 ft sidewalk, the outside dimensions are 10 ft by 14 ft. The sidewalk area is \(10\cdot14-8\cdot12=44\) square feet.
