Quick Reference

Volume measures the number of unit cubes needed to fill a three-dimensional solid. Use cubic units.

Right rectangular prism
\(V=lwh\)
Any prism or cylinder
\(V=Ah\)
\(A\) is the area of the base.
Pyramid or cone
\(V=\frac{1}{3}Ah\)
Sphere
\(V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)

Remember: The height is the perpendicular distance between the bases (or from the apex to the base). For an oblique prism, use the perpendicular height, not a slanted edge.

Common Volume Strategies

  • For a prism or cylinder, calculate the base area first, then multiply by the perpendicular height.
  • For a pyramid or cone, calculate one-third of the corresponding prism or cylinder volume.
  • For a composite solid, split it into familiar solids and add their volumes. For a missing or hollow portion, subtract its volume.
  • Keep exact answers involving \(\pi\) when appropriate, and include cubic units such as \(\text{cm}^3\), \(\text{ft}^3\), or \(\text{m}^3\).
