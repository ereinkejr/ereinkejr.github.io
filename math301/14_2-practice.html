Quick Reference

Reflection symmetry
A figure maps onto itself when reflected across a line of symmetry.
Translation symmetry
A figure or pattern maps onto itself after a slide.
Rotational symmetry
An order-\(n\) rotation maps a figure onto itself after a turn of \(360^\circ/n\).
Glide reflection
A reflection followed by a translation parallel to the line of reflection.
Congruent figures
Same shape and same size; one can be mapped to the other by an isometry.
Similar figures
Same shape; corresponding lengths are proportional, though sizes may differ.

An isometry preserves distances and angles. Reflections, translations, and rotations are isometries.

Similar triangles and measurement

When two triangles are similar, corresponding side lengths are proportional. For the shadow method:

\[\frac{\text{object height}}{\text{object shadow}}=\frac{\text{person height}}{\text{person shadow}}\]

For the mirror method in the notes:

\[\frac{\text{object height}}{\text{object to mirror}}=\frac{\text{eye height}}{\text{person to mirror}}\]

Tessellations

The interior angle of a regular polygon with \(n\) sides is

\[\theta=180^\circ\left(\frac{n-2}{n}\right).\]

For a regular polygon to tile a flat surface by itself, copies must fit around a point with no gaps or overlaps. Thus its interior angle must divide \(360^\circ\) evenly.

Equilateral triangle
Interior angle: \(60^\circ\). Six meet at a point.
Square
Interior angle: \(90^\circ\). Four meet at a point.
Regular hexagon
Interior angle: \(120^\circ\). Three meet at a point.

Additional Ideas

Möbius strip: a surface with one continuous side and one boundary curve.

Klein bottle: a non-orientable surface with no boundary; a familiar three-dimensional depiction passes through itself.

Similarity versus congruence: congruent figures have equal corresponding lengths, while similar figures have proportional corresponding lengths.
