Quick Reference

Probability is the study of chance. A sample space is the set of all possible outcomes. An event is a collection of one or more outcomes.
For any event \(A\), \[ 0 \leq P(A) \leq 1. \] An impossible event has probability \(0\); an event that is certain has probability \(1\).
Theoretical probability: When outcomes are equally likely, \[ P(A)=\frac{n(A)}{n(S)}=\frac{\text{number of outcomes in }A}{\text{number of outcomes in }S}. \]
Empirical (experimental) probability: \[ P(A)=\frac{\text{number of times }A\text{ occurred}}{\text{number of trials}}. \] This is also called relative frequency. With many repetitions, relative frequency tends to approach theoretical probability.
Complement: The complement \(\overline{E}\) means that event \(E\) does not happen. \[ P(\overline{E})=1-P(E). \]

A uniform probability model applies when all possible outcomes are equally likely. Do not assume outcomes are equally likely just because there are two possible results.

Sample Space Practice

For three coin tosses, each outcome is an ordered sequence of heads (H) and tails (T). The eight possible outcomes are:

HHHHHTHTHHTT
THHTHTTTHTTT

Exactly one tail occurs in HHT, HTH, and THH, so its probability is \(3/8\). Exactly three tails occurs only in TTT, so its probability is \(1/8\).

Important Distinction: Equally Likely Outcomes

For a fair die, the six faces are equally likely. For a coin, heads and tails are equally likely when the coin is fair. However, a basketball shot being made or missed, or a car starting or not starting, does not automatically create equally likely outcomes. The likelihood depends on the situation.
