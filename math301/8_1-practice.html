Essential ideas

For counting numbers \(A,B,C\), \(B\) is a factor (or divisor) of \(A\) when \(A=B\times C\). In this case, \(A\) is a multiple of \(B\), and we write \(B\mid A\).

To find every positive factor of \(A\), test possible divisors only through \(\sqrt{A}\). Every factor \(d<\sqrt A\) is paired with \(A/d>\sqrt A\). When \(A\) is a perfect square, the factor \(\sqrt A\) pairs with itself.
