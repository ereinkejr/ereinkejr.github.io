Quick reference

An integer is even if it is divisible by \(2\), and odd otherwise. Every integer has exactly one parity: even or odd. Algebraically, even integers have the form \(2k\), and odd integers have the form \(2k+1\), where \(k\in\mathbb Z\).

Pairing objects without leftovers determines evenness without counting. The ones digit determines parity because every other place value in base ten is divisible by \(2\).

Parity rules

\(\text{even}\pm\text{even}=\text{even}\), \(\text{odd}\pm\text{odd}=\text{even}\), and opposite parities give an odd sum or difference.

For products, \(\text{odd}\cdot\text{odd}=\text{odd}\); any product containing an even factor is even. Also, \(0=2(0)\), so zero is even.
