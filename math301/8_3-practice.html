Quick Reference

Use these tests to decide divisibility without long division.

By 2
The ones digit is \(0,2,4,6,\) or \(8\).
By 3
The sum of the digits is divisible by \(3\).
By 4
The number formed by the last two digits is divisible by \(4\).
By 5
The ones digit is \(0\) or \(5\).
By 6
The number is divisible by both \(2\) and \(3\).
By 9
The sum of the digits is divisible by \(9\).
By 10
The ones digit is \(0\).
By 11
The alternating sum of the digits is a multiple of \(11\), including \(0\).

Why do the tests work?

Writing a number in base ten gives \(N=a_0+10a_1+100a_2+\cdots\). Since \(10\equiv1\pmod 3\) and \(10\equiv1\pmod9\), the digit sum determines divisibility by \(3\) or \(9\). Since \(100\equiv0\pmod4\), only the last two digits matter for divisibility by \(4\). Since \(10\equiv-1\pmod{11}\), alternating digit sums determine divisibility by \(11\).
