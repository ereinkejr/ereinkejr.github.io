Quick Reference

A rational number can be written as \(\frac{a}{b}\), where \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\) and \(b\ne0\). A real number that is not rational is irrational.

Rational numbers have decimal expansions that terminate or eventually repeat. Irrational numbers have nonterminating, nonrepeating decimal expansions.

\(\mathbb N\subseteq\mathbb Z\subseteq\mathbb Q\subseteq\mathbb R\). Irrational numbers belong to \(\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q\). Every rational number is real, but not every real number is rational.

To convert a repeating block of \(k\) digits to a fraction, put the block over \(10^k-1\), then simplify: \(0.\overline{237}=\frac{237}{999}=\frac{79}{333}\).
