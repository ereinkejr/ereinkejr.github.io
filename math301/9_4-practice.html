Two Ways to Solve Word Problems

Many word problems can be solved by writing an algebraic equation and solving it. The same relationships can also be represented with a strip diagram. In either method, define the unknown, represent the relationships, and check that the answer makes sense.

Example: Candy bowl

A bowl contains 723 candies. There are twice as many green candies as red candies.

Let \(r\) be the number of red candies. Then green candies = \(2r\), so \(r+2r=723\). Thus \(3r=723\) and \(r=241\).

Green: \(r\)

Three equal units represent 723 candies; one unit is 241 candies.

Problem-Solving Checklist

  1. Identify what the question asks you to find.
  2. Choose a variable for an unknown quantity.
  3. Translate comparisons such as “twice as many,” “6 more,” or “one-half of the remainder.”
  4. Write an equation or build a strip diagram.
  5. Solve and check that the quantities add to the stated total.
  6. Include units and answer the question in a sentence.
