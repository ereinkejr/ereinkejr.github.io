Section 6.2 – Uniform Convergence of Sequences of Functions

MATH 382 · Real Analysis · Concepts, examples, and exam-style practice

Quick review

Exam 3 review emphasizes pointwise and uniform convergence, Section 6.2 exercises 1, 2, and 3(a,b), and proof of Theorem 6.2.6. These are original practice problems, not reproductions of textbook exercises.

Pointwise convergence

$f_n\to f$ pointwise on $E$ means: for every $x\in E$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $N=N(x,\varepsilon)$ such that $n\ge N$ implies $|f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon$. The threshold may depend on $x$.

Uniform convergence

$f_n\to f$ uniformly on $E$ means: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $N=N(\varepsilon)$ such that for every $n\ge N$ and every $x\in E$, $|f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon$. One threshold works for the entire domain.

Supremum criterion

When $f_n,f$ are bounded, uniform convergence is equivalent to $\sup_{x\in E}|f_n(x)-f(x)|\to0$. More generally, eventual uniform error bounds suffice even if early functions are unbounded.

Uniform Cauchy criterion

A sequence $f_n:E\to\mathbb R$ converges uniformly to a real-valued function if and only if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ such that $|f_n(x)-f_m(x)|<\varepsilon$ for every $x\in E$ and all $m,n\ge N$.

Uniform limit theorem (Theorem 6.2.6)

If $f_n$ is continuous on $E$ and $f_n\to f$ uniformly on $E$, then $f$ is continuous on $E$. This is a central Exam 3 proof target.

How to disprove uniform convergence

Find a fixed $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that for every $N$ some $n\ge N$ and $x_n\in E$ satisfy $|f_n(x_n)-f(x_n)|\ge\varepsilon_0$. The point $x_n$ is allowed to vary with $n$.

Worked examples

Uniform convergence by a bound

On $[0,2]$, $f_n(x)=x/(n+x)\to0$ uniformly because $0\le f_n(x)\le2/n$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N>2/\varepsilon$.

Pointwise but not uniform

On $[0,1]$, $f_n(x)=x^n$ converges pointwise to $f(x)=0$ for $0\le x<1$ and $f(1)=1$. Because $f$ is discontinuous at $1$, the convergence cannot be uniform.

Domain matters

For $f_n(x)=x/n$, convergence to zero is uniform on $[-4,4]$ since $\sup|f_n|=4/n$, but not uniform on $\mathbb R$ because for any $n$ the error $|x|/n$ is unbounded.

Moving points detect failure

On $(0,1)$, $f_n(x)=x^n\to0$ pointwise. Taking $x_n=2^{-1/n}$ gives $f_n(x_n)=1/2$ for every $n$, disproving uniform convergence.

Written-response and proof practice

Attempt each exercise before revealing its model solution.

Exercise 1: State both definitions

State pointwise and uniform convergence using quantifiers. Explain exactly where the index $N$ may depend on $x$.

Model solution

Pointwise: $\forall x\in E\,\forall\varepsilon>0\,\exists N(x,\varepsilon)\,\forall n\ge N:\ |f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon$. Uniform: $\forall\varepsilon>0\,\exists N(\varepsilon)\,\forall n\ge N\,\forall x\in E:\ |f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon$. The uniform threshold cannot depend on $x$.

Exercise 2: Uniform convergence on a bounded interval

Prove $f_n(x)=x^2/n$ converges uniformly to zero on $[-2,2]$.

Model solution

For all $x\in[-2,2]$, $|f_n(x)|\le4/n$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose an integer $N>4/\varepsilon$. Then $n\ge N$ gives $|f_n(x)|<\varepsilon$ for every $x$.

Exercise 3: Same sequence, different domain

Determine whether $f_n(x)=x^2/n$ converges uniformly to zero on $\mathbb R$.

Model solution

It converges pointwise because for fixed $x$, $x^2/n\to0$. It is not uniform: for each $n$ choose $x_n=\sqrt n$, giving $|f_n(x_n)|=1$. Thus the error cannot be uniformly less than $1/2$.

Exercise 4: Powers on a closed interval

Find the pointwise limit of $f_n(x)=x^n$ on $[0,1]$ and prove the convergence is not uniform.

Model solution

For $0\le x<1$, $x^n\to0$, while $f_n(1)=1$. The limit is discontinuous at $1$. Since each $f_n$ is continuous, the uniform limit theorem rules out uniform convergence.

Exercise 5: Powers on an open interval

Prove $x^n\to0$ pointwise but not uniformly on $(0,1)$ without appealing to discontinuity.

Model solution

For fixed $x\in(0,1)$, $x^n\to0$. Set $x_n=2^{-1/n}\in(0,1)$. Then $|f_n(x_n)|=1/2$ for every $n$. With $\varepsilon=1/4$, no uniform threshold works.

Exercise 6: Uniform Cauchy criterion

State the uniform Cauchy criterion and prove that uniform convergence implies it.

Model solution

For each $\varepsilon>0$, find $N$ such that $|f_n(x)-f_m(x)|<\varepsilon$ for all $x\in E$ and $m,n\ge N$. If $f_n\to f$ uniformly, choose $N$ making $|f_k(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon/2$ for all $k\ge N$ and all $x$. The triangle inequality yields $|f_n(x)-f_m(x)|<\varepsilon$.

Exercise 7: Prove the uniform limit theorem (6.2.6)

Assume $f_n:E\to\mathbb R$ are continuous at $c\in E$ and $f_n\to f$ uniformly. Prove $f$ is continuous at $c$.

Model solution

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. Uniform convergence gives $N$ such that $|f_N(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon/3$ for every $x\in E$. Continuity of $f_N$ at $c$ gives $\delta>0$ such that $x\in E$ and $|x-c|<\delta$ imply $|f_N(x)-f_N(c)|<\varepsilon/3$. Hence $|f(x)-f(c)|\le|f(x)-f_N(x)|+|f_N(x)-f_N(c)|+|f_N(c)-f(c)|<\varepsilon$. Therefore $f$ is continuous at $c$.

Exercise 8: Construct a counterexample

Give continuous functions on $[0,1]$ that converge pointwise to a discontinuous function. Explain why the convergence is not uniform.

Model solution

Take $f_n(x)=x^n$. Each is continuous. The pointwise limit is $0$ on $[0,1)$ and $1$ at $1$, which is discontinuous. The uniform limit theorem therefore proves the convergence is not uniform.
