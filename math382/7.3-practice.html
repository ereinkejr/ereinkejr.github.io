Section 7.3 – Integrating Functions with Discontinuities

MATH 382 Real Analysis · Abbott, Understanding Analysis, 2nd ed.

Exam 3 review lists Section 7.3 Exercises 1, 2, and 3. These are original companion problems rather than copies of the textbook exercises. The emphasis is on proving integrability from upper and lower sums.

Quick Review: Key Concepts

Discontinuities and integrability

A bounded function may be Riemann integrable even if it is discontinuous. In particular, a bounded function with finitely many discontinuities on $[a,b]$ is integrable.

The upper-minus-lower criterion

A bounded function $f$ is Riemann integrable exactly when for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a partition $P$ with $U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon$.

Finite changes

Changing a bounded Riemann integrable function at finitely many points does not affect integrability or the value of its integral.

Step functions

Every bounded step function with finitely many jumps is integrable. Its integral is the sum of (height) $\times$ (interval width), with signed heights.

Dense discontinuities

The Dirichlet function $1_{\mathbb Q}$ is not integrable. Thomae’s function, discontinuous at every rational and continuous at every irrational, is integrable with integral zero.

Further perspective

For bounded functions on a closed interval, the Lebesgue criterion says Riemann integrability is equivalent to the discontinuity set having measure zero. This is enrichment; the proofs below use partitions and upper/lower sums.

Visual intuition

Isolating a jump discontinuity

For a step function, only the narrow interval containing a jump contributes to the gap between upper and lower sums. Narrowing that interval makes the gap arbitrarily small.

Why the Dirichlet function fails

Rational and irrational points occur in every nontrivial subinterval. The oscillation is always 1, so no partition can shrink the upper-minus-lower gap.

Worked Examples

A single jump

$f(x)=0$ for $0\le x<1/2$, $f(x)=2$ for $1/2\le x\le1$. The only jump is at $1/2$; $\int_0^1 f=2(1/2)=1$.

A removable discontinuity

$g(x)=x$ except $g(1/3)=10$. The one-point change leaves $\int_0^1 g=1/2$ unchanged.

Nowhere-continuous example

$d(x)=1$ on rationals and $0$ on irrationals. For every partition, $U(d,P)=1$ and $L(d,P)=0$; no integral exists.

Infinitely many discontinuities

Thomae’s function has a discontinuity at every rational, but its integral is $0$. Rational values with large denominators are small; only finitely many rational points have small denominators.

Written Response and Proof Practice

Try each problem before expanding the model solution.

1. A single removable discontinuity

Let $f(x)=x^2$ for $x\ne 1/2$ and $f(1/2)=7$ on $[0,1]$. Prove integrability and find its integral.

Model solution

The function differs from the continuous function $g(x)=x^2$ at exactly one point. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose a partition that isolates $1/2$ in an interval of width $\delta$. Since $f$ is bounded, its extra oscillation contributes at most $C\delta$ to $U-L$ for some constant $C$. On the other intervals, the continuous function $g$ has arbitrarily small upper-minus-lower sum. Thus $f$ is integrable. Finite changes do not alter the integral, so $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=\int_0^1 x^2\,dx=1/3$.

2. A step function

Compute $\int_0^3 f$ where $f(x)=1$ for $0\le x<1$, $f(x)=-2$ for $1\le x<2$, and $f(x)=3$ for $2\le x\le3$. Explain why it is integrable.

Model solution

The only discontinuities are at $1$ and $2$, so the bounded function is Riemann integrable. By additivity, $\int_0^3 f=1(1)+(-2)(1)+3(1)=2$. The values assigned at the jump points do not change the integral.

3. Upper and lower sums

For $f(x)=0$ if $x<1/2$ and $f(x)=1$ if $x\ge1/2$, construct partitions $P_n$ with $U(f,P_n)-L(f,P_n)\to0$.

Model solution

For $n>2$, choose $P_n=\{0,1/2-1/n,1/2,1\}$. On $[0,1/2-1/n]$ and $[1/2,1]$, oscillation is zero (the latter is constant 1). Only $[1/2-1/n,1/2]$ has oscillation 1 and width $1/n$. Thus $U(f,P_n)-L(f,P_n)=1/n\to0$, proving integrability.

4. Dirichlet counterexample

Prove $d(x)=1$ for rational $x$ and $d(x)=0$ for irrational $x$ is not Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.

Model solution

Every nondegenerate subinterval contains both rational and irrational points. Consequently each subinterval has supremum 1 and infimum 0. For every partition $P$, $U(d,P)=1$ and $L(d,P)=0$. The gap is always 1, so the integrability criterion fails.

5. Finite modifications theorem

Prove that if bounded functions $f,g$ on $[a,b]$ agree except at finitely many points, then $f$ is integrable if and only if $g$ is, and their integrals agree.

Model solution

Let the exceptional set have $m$ points and choose a common bound $M$ on $|f|$ and $|g|$. Cover the exceptional points with finitely many partition intervals whose total length is less than $\delta$. Outside these intervals $f=g$. For a common refinement, the contribution to the difference between the upper and lower sums of the two functions is bounded by a constant multiple of $M\delta$. Choose $\delta$ arbitrarily small and use the upper-minus-lower criterion. The same estimate bounds the difference between the upper and lower integrals, so the integrals agree.

6. Thomae function

Define $t(0)=0$ and, for $x\in(0,1]$, set $t(x)=1/q$ if $x=p/q$ in lowest terms and $t(x)=0$ if $x$ is irrational. Show $t$ is Riemann integrable with integral 0.

Model solution

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N$ large so $1/N<\varepsilon/2$. There are finitely many reduced rationals in $[0,1]$ with denominator $q\le N$. Enclose them in partition intervals of total length less than $\varepsilon/2$. On every other interval, $0\le t\le1/N$; on the exceptional intervals, $0\le t\le1$. Thus $U(t,P)<\varepsilon$ after a suitable partition, while $L(t,P)=0$ because every interval contains irrationals. Therefore the integral is 0.

7. Continuity except at one point

Let $f$ be bounded on $[a,b]$ and continuous at every point except possibly $c\in(a,b)$. Prove $f$ is Riemann integrable.

Model solution

Choose a small closed interval around $c$ of length $\delta$. Its contribution to $U-L$ is at most $2M\delta$ where $|f|\le M$. On the two compact intervals outside this neighborhood, $f$ is continuous and therefore Riemann integrable, so choose partitions with arbitrarily small upper-minus-lower gaps. Combine the partitions and choose $\delta$ small enough that the total gap is less than any prescribed $\varepsilon>0$.

8. An oscillating discontinuity

Let $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=\sin(1/x)$ for $0<x\le1$. Show that $f$ is Riemann integrable, despite being discontinuous at $0$.

Model solution

The function is bounded by 1 and continuous on every $[\delta,1]$ with $\delta>0$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $\delta<\varepsilon/4$; the interval $[0,\delta]$ contributes at most $2\delta<\varepsilon/2$ to $U-L$. By continuity, partition $[\delta,1]$ so its gap is less than $\varepsilon/2$. The combined partition has $U-L<\varepsilon$, proving integrability.
