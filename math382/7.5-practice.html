Key concepts and theorems

FTC Part I: differentiation of an integral

If \(f\) is Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\) and continuous at \(c\in(a,b)\), then \(F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\) is differentiable at \(c\), with \(F\prime(c)=f(c)\). If \(f\) is continuous everywhere, \(F\prime=f\) throughout the interior.

FTC Part II: evaluation by antiderivatives

If \(f\) is Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\) and \(G\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), differentiable on \((a,b)\), with \(G\prime=f\) on \((a,b)\), then \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=G(b)-G(a)\).

Continuity of the accumulation function

If \(|f(x)|\le M\), then \(|F(y)-F(x)|=|\int_x^y f(t)\,dt|\le M|y-x|\). Thus \(F(x)=\int_a^x f\) is continuous even if \(f\) is discontinuous.

Why continuity matters in Part I

At a discontinuity \(c\), the derivative of \(F\) need not exist, or may exist but differ from \(f(c)\). The conclusion \(F\prime(c)=f(c)\) requires continuity at \(c\).

Integral mean value theorem

For continuous \(f\) on \([a,b]\), there exists \(c\in[a,b]\) such that \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=f(c)(b-a)\). This follows from minimum/maximum bounds and the intermediate value theorem.

Natural logarithm and exponential

Define \(L(x)=\int_1^x\frac{1}{t}\,dt\) for \(x>0\). The FTC gives \(L\prime(x)=1/x\); its inverse \(\exp\) satisfies \(\exp\prime(x)=\exp(x)\).

Visual intuition: accumulation and slope

The shaded area represents an accumulation function. When the upper limit moves a small distance, the added signed area is approximately the integrand height times that distance; continuity makes the approximation exact in the derivative limit.

\[\frac{F(x+h)-F(x)}{h}=\frac1h\int_x^{x+h}f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow f(x).\]

Worked examples

1. Differentiate an accumulation function

Let \(F(x)=\int_1^x (t^2+1)\,dt\). Find \(F\prime(x)\).

Solution. The integrand is continuous. By FTC Part I, \(F\prime(x)=x^2+1\). No integration is needed.

2. Evaluate an integral using an antiderivative

Compute \(\int_0^2 (3x^2+2x)\,dx\).

Solution. An antiderivative is \(G(x)=x^3+x^2\). FTC Part II yields \(G(2)-G(0)=8+4=12\).

3. A jump discontinuity

Let \(f(t)=0\) for \(t<1\) and \(f(t)=2\) for \(t\ge1\) on \([0,2]\). Find \(F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt\).

Solution. For \(0\le x\le1\), \(F(x)=0\). For \(1<x\le2\), \(F(x)=2(x-1)\). Thus \(F\) is continuous, but its left and right derivatives at \(1\) are \(0\) and \(2\), so \(F\prime(1)\) does not exist.

4. Logarithm from an integral

For \(x>0\), let \(L(x)=\int_1^x\frac1t\,dt\). Find \(L\prime(x)\) and \(L(1)\).

Solution. The integrand \(1/t\) is continuous for positive \(t\). Therefore \(L\prime(x)=1/x\), and \(L(1)=\int_1^1(1/t)\,dt=0\).

Exercise 1: Prove FTC Part I at a point

Let \(f\) be Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\) and continuous at \(c\in(a,b)\). Prove that \(F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\) satisfies \(F\prime(c)=f(c)\).

Show detailed model solution

For \(h\ne0\) with \(c+h\in[a,b]\), additivity gives \(\frac{F(c+h)-F(c)}{h}-f(c)=\frac1h\int_c^{c+h}(f(t)-f(c))\,dt\). By the integral bound, its absolute value is at most \(\sup_{t\text{ between }c\text{ and }c+h}|f(t)-f(c)|\). Continuity at \(c\) makes this supremum tend to zero as \(h\to0\). Hence \(F\prime(c)=f(c)\).

Exercise 2: Prove FTC Part II

Assume \(f\) is Riemann integrable and \(G\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), differentiable on \((a,b)\), with \(G\prime=f\). Show \(\int_a^b f=G(b)-G(a)\).

Show detailed model solution

Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose a partition \(P\) with \(U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon\). On each \([x_{i-1},x_i]\), the Mean Value Theorem gives \(G(x_i)-G(x_{i-1})=f(c_i)(x_i-x_{i-1})\) for some \(c_i\) in the open subinterval. Summing telescopes to \(G(b)-G(a)\), which lies between \(L(f,P)\) and \(U(f,P)\). The integral lies between these bounds too. Their difference is therefore less than \(\varepsilon\). Since \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, equality follows.

Exercise 3: Continuity without continuity of the integrand

Prove that if \(f\) is bounded and Riemann integrable, then \(F(x)=\int_a^x f\) is continuous.

Show detailed model solution

Choose \(M\) with \(|f|\le M\). For any \(x,y\in[a,b]\), additivity and the comparison inequality yield \(|F(y)-F(x)|=|\int_x^y f(t)\,dt|\le M|y-x|\) (with reversed limits handled by sign). Given \(\varepsilon>0\), take \(\delta=\varepsilon/(M+1)\). Then \(|x-y|<\delta\) implies \(|F(y)-F(x)|<\varepsilon\).

Exercise 4: Analyze a jump discontinuity

Let \(f(t)=1\) for \(0\le t<1\) and \(f(t)=3\) for \(1\le t\le2\). Find \(F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt\), and determine where \(F\) is differentiable.

Show detailed model solution

For \(0\le x\le1\), \(F(x)=x\). For \(1<x\le2\), \(F(x)=1+3(x-1)=3x-2\). Both formulas give \(F(1)=1\), so \(F\) is continuous. On \((0,1)\), \(F\prime=1\); on \((1,2)\), \(F\prime=3\). The one-sided derivatives at \(1\) differ, so \(F\prime(1)\) does not exist.

Exercise 5: Chain rule with variable upper limit

Find the derivative of \(H(x)=\int_0^{\sin x}(1+t^2)\,dt\).

Show detailed model solution

Write \(A(u)=\int_0^u(1+t^2)\,dt\), so \(A\prime(u)=1+u^2\) by FTC Part I. The chain rule gives \(H\prime(x)=A\prime(\sin x)\cos x=(1+\sin^2 x)\cos x\).

Exercise 6: Prove the integral mean value theorem

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), \(a<b\), prove there is \(c\in[a,b]\) with \(\int_a^b f=f(c)(b-a)\).

Show detailed model solution

By the Extreme Value Theorem, \(m=\min f\) and \(M=\max f\) exist. Comparison gives \(m(b-a)\le\int_a^b f\le M(b-a)\). Dividing by \(b-a>0\), the average value \(A=(b-a)^{-1}\int_a^b f\) lies in \([m,M]\). By the Intermediate Value Theorem, \(f(c)=A\) for some \(c\in[a,b]\).

Exercise 7: Construct logarithm using FTC

For \(x>0\), define \(L(x)=\int_1^x 1/t\,dt\). Prove that \(L\) is strictly increasing and that \(L\prime(x)=1/x\).

Show detailed model solution

Because \(1/t\) is continuous on every closed interval in \((0,\infty)\), FTC Part I gives \(L\prime(x)=1/x>0\). Thus \(L\) is strictly increasing by the Mean Value Theorem. Alternatively, if \(0<u<v\), then \(L(v)-L(u)=\int_u^v(1/t)\,dt>0\) by comparison with the positive constant \(1/v\).

Exercise 8: Derive the derivative of the exponential

Assume \(L(x)=\int_1^x1/t\,dt\) maps \((0,\infty)\) bijectively onto \(\mathbb R\). Let \(E=L^{-1}\). Prove \(E\prime(y)=E(y)\).

Show detailed model solution

Since \(L\prime(x)=1/x\ne0\), the inverse function differentiation rule gives \(E\prime(y)=1/L\prime(E(y))=1/(1/E(y))=E(y)\). In particular \(E(0)=1\), because \(L(1)=0\).
