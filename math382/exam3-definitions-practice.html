Quick review · All 15 definitions

Pointwise convergence

\(f_n\to f\) pointwise on \(A\) means: for every \(x\in A\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(N=N(x,\varepsilon)\) such that \(n\ge N\Rightarrow |f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon\).

The index may depend on the point x.

Uniform convergence

\(f_n\to f\) uniformly on \(A\) means: for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(N=N(\varepsilon)\) such that for every \(x\in A\) and all \(n\ge N\), \(|f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon\).

One N works simultaneously for all x.

Power series

A series of the form \(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(x-c)^n\), where \(c\) is its center and the \(a_n\) are coefficients.

Convergence depends on x.

Taylor series

For a function with derivatives of every order at \(c\), its formal Taylor series is \(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(c)}{n!}(x-c)^n\).

The Taylor series need not equal the function away from c.

Partition

A partition of \([a,b]\) is a finite set \(P=\{x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\) with \(a=x_0

Subintervals are [x_{k-1},x_k].

mₖ and Mₖ

For a bounded function \(f\) on \([a,b]\), \(m_k=\inf\{f(x):x\in[x_{k-1},x_k]\}\) and \(M_k=\sup\{f(x):x\in[x_{k-1},x_k]\}\).

Infimum and supremum need not be attained.

Lower sum

\(L(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}m_k(x_k-x_{k-1})\).

Uses the infimum on each subinterval.

Upper sum

\(U(f,P)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_k(x_k-x_{k-1})\).

Uses the supremum on each subinterval.

Upper integral

For bounded \(f\) on \([a,b]\), the upper integral is \(\overline{\int_a^b}f=\inf_P U(f,P)\), with the infimum over all partitions.

Infimum of upper sums.

Lower integral

For bounded \(f\) on \([a,b]\), the lower integral is \(\underline{\int_a^b}f=\sup_P L(f,P)\), with the supremum over all partitions.

Supremum of lower sums.

Refinement

A partition \(Q\) refines \(P\) if \(P\subseteq Q\); it contains every point of \(P\) and possibly more.

Refining increases lower sums and decreases upper sums.

Riemann integral

If the upper and lower integrals of a bounded function on \([a,b]\) are equal, their common value is its Riemann integral \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\).

Equality of upper and lower integrals.

Integrable function

A bounded function \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R\) is Riemann integrable when \(\sup_P L(f,P)=\inf_P U(f,P)\); equivalently, for each \(\varepsilon>0\) some partition satisfies \(U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon\).

The gap criterion is especially useful in proofs.

Natural logarithm

For \(x>0\), \(\log x=\int_1^x \frac{1}{t}\,dt\).

This is the integral definition used in real analysis.

Natural exponential

The natural exponential \(\exp:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty)\) is the inverse of the strictly increasing function \(\log:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\).

Thus exp(log x)=x for x>0 and log(exp y)=y.

Written-response and proof practice

Write your response first, then expand the model solution.

1. State pointwise and uniform convergence with all quantifiers.

Show detailed model solution
Pointwise: \(\forall x\in A\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N=N(x,\varepsilon)\ \forall n\ge N:\ |f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon\). Uniform: \(\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N=N(\varepsilon)\ \forall x\in A\ \forall n\ge N:\ |f_n(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon\). The order of the quantifiers is decisive.

2. On [0,1], show that \(f_n(x)=x^n\) converges pointwise but not uniformly.

Show detailed model solution
For \(0\le x<1\), \(x^n\to0\); at \(x=1\), \(x^n=1\). Thus the pointwise limit is \(f(x)=0\) for \(x<1\), \(f(1)=1\). For each n, \(\sup_{0\le x<1}|x^n-f(x)|=1\), so the uniform error does not tend to zero. Hence convergence is not uniform.

3. Give the general forms of a power series and a Taylor series centered at c. Explain one key distinction.

Show detailed model solution
A power series is \(\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n(x-c)^n\), with arbitrary prescribed coefficients. The Taylor series of a smooth function f is \(\sum_{n=0}^\infty f^{(n)}(c)(x-c)^n/n!\). Taylor coefficients are determined by derivatives. Even if the Taylor series converges, it need not converge to f.

4. For \(f(x)=x\) on [0,2] and \(P=\{0,1,2\}\), calculate all mₖ, Mₖ, lower sum, and upper sum.

Show detailed model solution
On [0,1], \(m_1=0,M_1=1\); on [1,2], \(m_2=1,M_2=2\). Both widths equal 1. Hence \(L(f,P)=0(1)+1(1)=1\), while \(U(f,P)=1(1)+2(1)=3\).

5. Refine the partition in the previous problem to \(Q=\{0, frac12,1, frac32,2\}\). Compare the sums.

Show detailed model solution
The four lower values are \(0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32\), and the upper values are \(\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2\). Each width is \(1/2\). Thus \(L(f,Q)=\tfrac12(0+\tfrac12+1+\tfrac32)=\tfrac32\) and \(U(f,Q)=\tfrac12(\tfrac12+1+\tfrac32+2)=\tfrac52\). We see \(1\le\tfrac32\le\tfrac52\le3\).

6. State the definitions of the upper and lower integrals and the criterion for integrability.

Show detailed model solution
For bounded f, \(\underline{\int_a^b}f=\sup_P L(f,P)\) and \(\overline{\int_a^b}f=\inf_P U(f,P)\). The function is Riemann integrable precisely when these are equal. Equivalently, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a partition P such that \(U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon\).

7. Prove that refining a partition cannot decrease the lower sum or increase the upper sum.

Show detailed model solution
It suffices to insert one point c into a subinterval [u,v]. The infimum on each smaller interval is at least the infimum on [u,v], so the sum of the two new lower rectangles is at least \(m(v-u)\). Similarly, each smaller supremum is at most the old supremum M, so the two new upper rectangles total at most \(M(v-u)\). Repeating for every inserted point proves \(L(f,P)\le L(f,Q)\) and \(U(f,Q)\le U(f,P)\).

8. Define the natural logarithm and exponential, and derive \(\log 1=0\) and \(\exp(0)=1\).

Show detailed model solution
For \(x>0\), define \(\log x=\int_1^x(1/t)\,dt\). Therefore \(\log1=\int_1^1(1/t)\,dt=0\). Since log is strictly increasing and maps (0,∞) onto ℝ, its inverse exp exists. Applying the inverse to \(\log1=0\) yields \(\exp(0)=1\).
